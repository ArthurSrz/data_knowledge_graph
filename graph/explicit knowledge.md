[[knowledge]] that has been articulated, codified and stored in some medium.

The conversion is the *externalization* step of the [[SECI model of knowledge dimensions]].

## Three grades

Once written down, a statement does not carry the same weight depending on who backs it. Three grades, from weakest to strongest:

| Grade | What it is | Kind of statement | Who backs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Attributed position | What one person says is true or should be done | [[positive statement]] or proposal | One source, named in the [[provenance]] link |
| 2. Current-state fact | What is true today, checked by someone other than the source | [[positive statement]] | Source + an independent reviewer |
| 3. Adopted rule | What must be done from now on, decided by whoever has authority | [[normative statement]], [[business rule]] | A decision, dated, with its owner |

Rules:
- A statement only moves up a grade through a new event (a review, a decision). Rewriting it in impersonal form does not change its grade.
- A grade-1 proposal that contradicts a grade-2 fact is not a [[logical contradiction]]: it is the gap between what should be and what is (see [[normative statement]]).
- A correction from the source itself stays grade 1. Only a reviewer moves it to grade 2.

**Example:** an expert writes down how a piece of equipment must be handled, then corrects what an agent misunderstood. Both stay grade 1 until a second expert reviews them (grade 2). Only a decision signed by the person in charge turns them into a rule (grade 3).
