[[model]] of [[knowledge]] creation that explains how [[tacit knowledge]] and [[explicit knowledge]] are converted into organizational [[knowledge]]. Developed by Ikujiro Nonaka in 1990, later refined with Hirotaka Takeuchi popularised by *The [[Knowledge]]-Creating Company* (Nonaka & Takeuchi, 1995).

## Four modes of [[knowledge]] conversion

[[Knowledge]] is created through the interaction between tacit and [[explicit knowledge]], which gives four conversions (the "SECI" acronym):

| Mode | Conversion | How it happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Socialization** | tacit → tacit | Shared experience and physical proximity: observation, imitation, practice, apprenticeship, brainstorming with colleagues, direct contact with customers. |
| **Externalization** | tacit → explicit | [[Tacit knowledge]] is crystallized into concepts, images or written documents, so others can interpret it and build on it. |
| **[[Combination]]** | explicit → explicit | Different explicit sources, from inside or outside the organization, are collected, organized, edited and merged into new [[explicit knowledge]], then disseminated; supported by networks and large databases. |
| **Internalization** | explicit → tacit | An individual absorbs [[explicit knowledge]] by [[learning]]-by-doing and reflection until it becomes their own know-how, and thus an organizational asset. |

The four modes form a **spiral of [[knowledge]] creation**: a continuous cycle in which each turn strengthens the interaction between tacit and [[explicit knowledge]].

**Ba** (Nonaka & Konno): the shared [[context]], roughly "place", in which [[knowledge]] is shared, created and used. It unifies physical space (an office), virtual space (e-mail) and mental space (shared ideas). Hors Wikidata: no QID found for this sense of *ba*.

## Acceptance and criticism

The [[model]] is widely known and has reached paradigmatic status in [[knowledge management]]. Its strengths: it treats [[knowledge]] creation as dynamic, and it gives a framework for managing those processes.

Criticisms:
- It rests on studies of Japanese firms, which rely heavily on [[tacit knowledge]] and lifetime employment.
- It looks linear: can the spiral skip steps or run backwards?
- Gourlay (2003, 2006): if [[tacit knowledge]] is the source of new [[knowledge]], why must conversion start with socialization? New [[knowledge]] can just as well start with [[combination]], a creative synthesis of explicit sources.
- It does not explain how new ideas and solutions are actually developed in practice.

Related concepts named by Wikipedia (not yet in the [[graph]]): Four stages of competence, I-Space, organizational [[learning]].

## What [[Clarifeye]] does

What Clara ([[clarifeye]]) does covers two of the four conversions:
- **Externalization**: [[tacit knowledge]] from interviews → [[explicit knowledge]] written as impersonal [[business rule]]s with [[provenance]];
- **[[Combination]]**: explicit sources merged into the method and registers ([[systematisation]], [[information integration]]).

Gourlay's criticism fits [[Clarifeye]]: a curation pass often *starts* with [[combination]] (reference documentation + vocabulary export + register), and the interview that follows is the externalization step. The spiral does not have to begin with socialization.

The two conversions fail in different ways :
- an externalization fails when a single [[person]]'s [[normative statement]] is written as an impersonal [[rule]] and loses its [[provenance]];
- a [[combination]] fails when the merged claim never reaches the friction register, or when two registers drift apart ([[single source of truth]]).
